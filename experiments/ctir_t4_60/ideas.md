# CTIR ideas

## IDEA-CTIR-001 — Dynamic current-state isospectral update redirection

- Status: v1 mechanism supported; behavioral claim unresolved because the available vanilla comparator is not schedule-paired
- Created/updated: 2026-09-02 Asia/Shanghai
- Question: during T4 steps 0–60, can the actual Puzzle AdamW update be orthogonally redirected away from the current Navigation-sensitive tangent while retaining at least 90% of its Puzzle descent proxy?
- Mechanism: estimate rank-8 left/right Navigation frames from 32 fixed old-task probes every five steps; use rank-8 raw-delta frames only to construct full-dimensional orthogonal maps; apply those maps to the complete FP32 AdamW master delta.
- Falsifiable signal: Navigation recovery at step60 at least +5 points with Puzzle cost at most 3 points, plus systematic old-harm reduction, mostly-safe new-descent ratios, numerical isospectrality, and nontrivial beta choices.
- Evidence from `EXP-CTIR-003`: the implementation passed beta-zero and exact full-spectrum gates. In the formal run, beta was nonzero on 36/59 nonzero updates, all 36 predicted-harmful updates were improved, their summed harm proxy fell 80.4%, and all Puzzle one-step descent ratios remained at least 0.9008. At step 60, Navigation was +4.60 points vs historical vanilla, but Puzzle was -10.25 points; both predeclared historical-reference gates failed. This is not a causal CTIR effect estimate: CTIR used a 60-step cosine schedule with 6 warmup steps, whereas historical vanilla was the first 60 steps of a 300-step schedule with 30 warmup steps. See `summary.md`.
- Counter-evidence/risks: the teacher-forced Navigation gradient may not predict behavioral retention; preserving delta spectrum does not guarantee Puzzle function preservation; dynamic tangent refresh and ZeRO-3 full-matrix interception are expensive.
- Minimal experiment: `EXP-CTIR-003`, after `EXP-CTIR-001/002` correctness gates. Evaluate step0/10/.../60 on MedBookVQA, Navigation, We-Math2, and Puzzle.
- Confounders: first round uses historical rather than paired same-seed baseline at steps 0/30/60. The LR sum over steps 1–60 was `1.50e-4` for CTIR versus `2.2591e-4` for historical vanilla; CTIR had 4.08x the vanilla LR mass in steps 1–10 but only 0.21x in steps 31–60. Paired Vanilla/Projection/Static-IsoRotate remain unrun.
- Interpretation: current Navigation geometry is directionally informative, but the present controller's local first-order quantities do not predict the observed long-horizon full-CoT NLL. The existing performance comparison cannot establish whether CTIR itself impaired Puzzle acquisition. Average tangent drift was modest (step-55 alignment 0.9707), with concentrated drift in a subset of matrices (minimum 0.8530), so the necessity of frequent refresh remains unresolved.
- Next decision: do not scale v1 to 300 steps. A causal behavioral diagnosis requires one paired 60-step vanilla control with the exact CTIR scheduler/seed/config. Independently, the orthogonal lift should be corrected as described in IDEA-CTIR-002 before treating beta as a rotation strength.

## IDEA-CTIR-002 — Gauge-fixed local isospectral transport

- Status: implemented locally on 2026-09-03; full 300-step T4 run pending
- Created/updated: 2026-09-02 Asia/Shanghai
- Question: can CTIR preserve the intended rank-8 frame redirection while eliminating uncontrolled action on the complete raw-delta tail?
- Mechanism: regard matrices with the same singular spectrum as a two-sided orthogonal orbit. Move the dominant raw frames away from the old-task frames on the Grassmann manifold, then use the identity-connected direct rotation (the norm-closest orthogonal lift) on each side. Equivalently, use low-rank skew generators and an exponential/geodesic map. Apply the resulting maps to the complete raw optimizer delta, so no singular value is truncated.
- Falsifiable hypothesis: compared with the current sequential-Householder lift under the same target frames, a direct-rotation lift preserves the identical redirected rank-8 component and the identical full singular spectrum, while making actual update displacement continuous in beta and reducing arbitrary tail motion enough to recover Puzzle plasticity.
- Evidence: `orthogonal_redirect.py` logs `||U_target-U||_F` as rotation strength but constructs a product of Householder reflectors. For a nontrivial Householder, `||H-I||_2=2` regardless of target-frame displacement. In a targeted synthetic complete-delta diagnostic, at beta `0.25` the Householder and direct-rotation lifts had maximum operator distances `2.000` and `0.143`, complete-delta relative changes `0.485` and `0.181`, and tail relative changes `0.989` and `0.076`; both preserved the complete spectrum to numerical precision. At beta `0.5`, tail changes were `0.980` and `0.178`. The intended dominant rank-8 redirected component is the same for both lifts.
- Related within-run evidence: over steps 1–55 the cached protected-matrix first-order proxy predicted a Navigation probe-NLL change of `-0.00911`, whereas measured full-CoT NLL changed by `+0.07628` (`+4.39%`). The first 5-step interval was close (`+0.00227` predicted vs `+0.00281` observed), but steps 15–20 had opposite signs (`-0.00031` predicted vs `+0.03206` observed). This is consistent with an overly nonlocal lift, but unprotected parameters, curvature, and five-step tangent staleness remain alternative contributors.
- Theoretical distinction: the existing `orth[(I-beta QQ^T)U]` target is a first-order Grassmann retraction away from the sensitive subspace; that part is geometrically coherent. The underspecified step is the lift from the target frame to a full ambient orthogonal map. Sequential Householder is text-compatible with the frozen plan, so this is a gauge/locality defect in the method specification rather than a failure to preserve spectra.
- Implemented transport: retain the existing beta-specific Grassmann target frames, but replace the Householder lift with the identity-connected principal-plane direct rotation. This is the lower-cost revision and isolates the diagnosed gauge defect. The complete raw delta is still transformed on both sides; rank 8 remains a frame-control rank, not an update truncation rank.
- Implemented controller: retain one global beta for the first v2 run. Enforce the existing new-task floor `new_descent >= 0.90 * raw_new_descent`, then lexicographically minimize positive old-task constraint violation `max(old_harm, 0)` and actual complete-update Frobenius distance. Thus, once `old_harm <= 0` is attainable, the controller chooses the closest such update rather than maximizing negative old harm. If the discrete grid has no jointly feasible point, it reports and minimizes residual old-task violation before distance. No arbitrary update-distance cap was introduced.
- Rationale for deferring per-matrix beta: it would mix the geometry correction with a new multiple-choice constrained allocation problem. The 300-step run logs all global candidates and actual update distances so a later per-matrix controller can be justified from evidence rather than added speculatively.
- Minimal experiment: run the v2 method for the complete 300-step T4 stage with the original 300-step vanilla trajectory as the schedule-paired comparator. Retain rank/probes/refresh/beta/seed/training configuration, and log actual `||delta_tilde-delta_raw||_F/||delta_raw||_F`, update cosine, principal angles, controller feasibility, and every global-beta candidate. Do not simultaneously tune rank or refresh interval.
- Prior-art boundary: GEM selects the Euclidean closest gradient satisfying old-task constraints; Stiefel/Grassmann direct rotations provide the analogous identity-connected geometry; recent Pion/GIFT methods preserve the spectrum of the weight matrix itself. CTIR instead preserves the spectrum of a proposed optimizer delta, so its closest adjacent literature does not make the update-transport formulation identical. Novelty still requires a dedicated literature review.
